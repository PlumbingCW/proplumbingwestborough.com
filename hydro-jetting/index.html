Emergency Hydro Jetting in Westborough, MA

What a water jet does that a cable cannot

A cable is a spinning blade on a coil. It cuts a channel through a blockage, and the channel is often only an inch or two wide. The rest of the buildup stays on the pipe wall and traps the next load of debris.

A jet nozzle sends water backward and sideways at high pressure while it is pulled up the line. The water scours the entire circumference of the pipe and flushes the debris toward the cleanout or the main. The pipe ends up near its original diameter, which is why the clog takes much longer to return.

Water volume matters as much as pressure. A jetter that moves a large flow of water carries debris out of the line instead of pushing it into a bend.

We snaked it and it got stuck: what that usually means

A cable that jams at a fixed distance, say 60 feet, usually hit something that does not give. Common causes are a root mass, a heavy grease cap or a collapsed section of pipe. The cable fails to pass because it is turning in place.

That is the point at which a camera should go in. If the pipe is intact, a jet with a root-cutting nozzle can usually pass the obstruction. If the camera shows a broken or offset pipe, jetting would only push water into the surrounding soil, and the job becomes sewer line repair instead.

Backup tonight: what to do before the jetter arrives

Hydro jetting is the right answer for many repeat backups, but the first priority is limiting damage while dispatch is on the way.

  • Stop every water source in the house. Wastewater has nowhere to go and will keep rising at the lowest drain.
  • Cover floor drains in the basement with a heavy lid or a weighted board if they are bubbling, and move stored items off the floor.
  • Find the sewer cleanout, often a capped pipe near the foundation or in the basement. Tell the dispatcher where it is. Do not open it yourself, since a full line can discharge forcefully.
  • Keep pets and children away from any standing water, and wash your hands after touching anything wet.
  • Photograph the damage for your records and for any insurance conversation.

Roots, grease and scale: three coatings, three nozzles

Roots enter at joints looking for moisture. A jet with a cutting nozzle trims the fine roots flush with the pipe wall, but it does not stop the joint from leaking. Expect roots to return in a year or two unless the joint is repaired.

Grease forms a waxy ring that narrows the pipe a little each year. A flat-spray nozzle at moderate pressure strips it without gouging the pipe. Scale from hard mineral deposits is the toughest, and it may need several passes at different angles.

Which of the three you have can be seen on the camera. That is why a plumber who skips the camera is guessing at the nozzle.

Pipes that should not be jetted

High pressure is not safe in every pipe. A responsible plumber declines to jet when any of these apply, and tells you why.

  • Cracked, broken or offset sections that the camera has already found, because water will blast into the soil.
  • Thin-walled or heavily corroded cast iron, which can flake and release a plug of debris downstream.
  • Old clay lines with loose joints that a jet may separate.
  • Pipes that have been lined with a failed liner, where the pressure can strip the liner.
  • Fixtures without a proper cleanout, where the access point is a toilet flange that cannot take the hose.

Restaurants and shops along the Route 9 corridor

Route 9 carries much of Westborough's commercial activity, and food service lines are heavy users of jetting. Kitchen drains discharge fats, oils and grease that build up in the shared lateral faster than in a house.

How often jetting makes sense

A house with a sound pipe and a one-time clog may never need a second jet. A home with mature trees close to the sewer lateral, or with a long run of old iron, may benefit from a jet every one to three years, depending on the camera.

Keep the camera recording. It lets you compare the pipe wall year over year and decide when a cleaning has stopped working and a repair is the better spend.
