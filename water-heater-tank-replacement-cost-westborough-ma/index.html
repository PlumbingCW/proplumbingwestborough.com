Gas or electric: the first fork

A like-for-like swap is the cheapest path. If you move from electric to gas, the job adds a gas line and a vent, and each is licensed work. Moving from gas to a heat pump unit removes the burner and vent but may need a dedicated electrical circuit and clearance for airflow.

A heat pump water heater is a real option for some homes. It costs more to buy and often saves energy over time. Our water heater replacement page explains the choices in more detail.

Size matters, but not the way people expect

A household of two does not need the same tank as a household of five. A 40 gallon tank is common for small households and 50 gallons for larger ones. Going up a size adds a modest amount to the price. Going too small leaves you running out of hot water, which costs more in daily annoyance than the saved dollars.

First hour rating, not the gallon count alone, is the number that tells you how much hot water a heater delivers in a busy hour. Ask for it when comparing models.

The parts nobody mentions until the bill

A good quote lists these items by name. If one is missing, ask why.

  • A thermal expansion tank when the system is closed by a check valve or pressure reducing valve.
  • A drain pan with a drain line where a leak would damage floors.
  • New flexible water connectors and shutoff valves.
  • A temperature and pressure relief valve with a proper discharge pipe.
  • A venting kit or liner for a gas unit.
  • A gas shutoff and sediment trap on the gas line.

Emergency timing changes the quote

A tank that is leaking now is an emergency. Shut off the cold water supply to the heater, turn off power at the breaker or set the gas control to off, and call. Do not wait for a drain to fill the basement.

After-hours work costs more than a scheduled appointment, because dispatch, travel, and the technician's time are all premium. If the leak is small and you can manage it, a scheduled visit saves money. If water is spreading, urgency comes first.

  • Close the cold water shutoff valve on the heater.
  • Switch off the breaker or set the gas valve to off.
  • Open a hot faucet to relieve pressure only if it is safe.
  • Move belongings away from the water path.

Repair or replace: a quick rule

If the tank itself is leaking, replacement is the answer. If the tank is sound and a part failed, repair might make sense, especially under about eight years old. Our water heater repair page explains the usual repairable failures.

Heaters usually serve eight to twelve years. After that, rust and sediment catch up. If yours is in that range and a repair would cost a large share of a replacement, replacement tends to be the sounder choice.

How to compare two quotes

Put the quotes side by side and compare the model, the first hour rating, the warranty length, and the parts list. A lower number often leaves out the expansion tank, the permit, or the haul-away. Those items appear later as extras.

Ask each company who pulls the permit and who is licensed to do the gas or electrical work. Ask what happens if the inspector requires a change. A clear answer to those questions says more about a quote than the total at the bottom.

Westborough, MA note: permits and the town water

Permit rules vary, so ask the Westborough Building Department before the work starts. Massachusetts plumbing is regulated under 248 CMR 10.00 and gas under 248 CMR 4.00, and the Board of State Examiners of Plumbers and Gas Fitters licenses the people who do the work.

Westborough's water comes from town wells and the Westboro Reservoir per MassDEP's older source water assessment, which lists corrosion control. A tank's anode rod and sediment buildup depend on your local water, so ask the Westborough Water Department for its current annual report and test your tap before choosing a model.
