Water is pouring out: do this before the plumber arrives

A split pipe under pressure moves a large volume of water fast. Your goal in the first minutes is to cut the supply, protect electrical areas and slow the spread. Everything else can wait for the plumber.

Start with the main shutoff, which is usually where the water line enters the house, often near the meter. Turn the handle fully. If the valve is a round wheel, turn it clockwise. Then open the lowest faucet in the house to drain pressure from the lines, and open the highest one to let air in so the pipes empty faster.

  • Close the main water valve completely.
  • Switch off power to wet rooms at the breaker if the panel is dry and you can reach it safely. Never touch a switch or outlet while standing in water.
  • Open a low faucet and a high faucet to drain the lines.
  • Turn off the water heater power or gas control so the tank does not run dry and overheat.
  • Move valuables, rugs and electronics, and photograph the damage for your insurer.
  • Call call (508) 389-4045 and tell dispatch which room is wet.

Why pipes split in the first place

Pipes burst for three main reasons: freezing water, high pressure, and age. Ice is the loudest cause in a New England winter because water expands as it freezes. The split usually happens not where the ice sits but a little downstream, where pressure builds between the ice plug and a closed faucet.

Age shows up differently. Corroded steel, brittle plastic, and joints that have flexed with years of heating and cooling develop cracks that fail under normal pressure. Surges from a fast-closing valve or a failed pressure regulator can finish off a weak spot. We look for the cause during the repair, since fixing only the split section invites the next one.

Frozen pipes: thaw carefully or call before it splits

Westborough sits in the Sudbury and Assabet river headwaters, and winter cold settles hard in this part of Worcester County. Pipes in exterior walls, garages, crawl spaces and unheated basements are the usual casualties. If a faucet runs a trickle or stops, a pipe may be frozen but not yet split.

Thaw only when you can see the pipe and no water is leaking. Never use a torch or open flame, since that can ignite framing and damage joints. If you see a crack, a bulge or any wet spot, do not thaw. Close the main and call.

  • Open the faucet served by the frozen line so melting water can leave.
  • Warm the pipe slowly with a hair dryer, a heating pad or warm towels, starting nearest the faucet.
  • Keep heat on until full pressure returns, then check every joint for drips.
  • Open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls so room heat reaches the pipes.
  • If you cannot find the frozen section, or the pipe is already split, shut the main valve and call.

Copper pipe repair: sweated joints, pinholes and splits

Copper has served American homes for generations and still earns its place. A freeze split is often a clean lengthwise crack that a coupling repairs well. Pinholes are different. They are tiny leaks that develop from the inside out, sometimes in the same few feet of pipe.

When pinholes repeat, patching one only moves the problem. We check the surrounding pipe, the age of the run and any pattern in the water. Westborough water treatment includes corrosion control according to the MassDEP source water assessment, but that report is older, so ask for the current annual report and share it with your plumber if pinholes recur.

Cracked drain and waste pipe repair

Not every burst is on the supply side. A cracked drain pipe leaks wastewater instead of clean water, usually with a smell or a damp spot below a bathroom or kitchen. Causes include ground movement, impact during other work, and joints that have worn out in old cast iron.

We pressure test or camera inspect the line to confirm the break, then cut out the cracked section and replace it with new pipe and the right couplings. If the damaged run feeds a toilet or a main stack, we protect the rest of the system by making sure the new section is properly supported and sloped.

What happens after the water stops

Once the break is isolated, we open the minimum amount of wall to expose the damaged section, replace it, and test the repair under full pressure before closing anything. You will see the pressure hold on a gauge, not just hear us say it is fine. Then we bring the water back on slowly so the pipes do not hammer.

Drying matters as much as the repair. Wet drywall and insulation that stay damp for more than a day or two risk mold. We tell you what we see, but cleanup and drying belong to a restoration company or your own crew, and your insurer may direct you to one.

Pipes under a house that was built long before plumbing codes

Westborough was incorporated in 1717, and the town still has older buildings in places like the Vintonville and West Main Street historic districts. Homes of that age may carry pipe from several eras inside one wall, including iron, steel and copper that have been joined over time. Mixed materials leak where they meet, and old fittings may not match modern parts.

The practical result is that a burst in an older home often needs more than a patch. We photograph what we find, explain which sections are sound, and let you decide whether to repair only the break or replace a longer run. In historic buildings we also try to limit cuts in plaster and trim. For longer replacement work, see our page on repiping.
