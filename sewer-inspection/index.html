Sewer Camera Inspection in Westborough, MA

A sewer camera inspection in Westborough, MA puts a waterproof camera inside your drain line and shows what is wrong, if anything, on a screen you can watch. The visit is recorded, so you can keep the footage.

It is the planned way to learn the condition of a lateral before a purchase, a repair or a remodel, and it is the first step after any repeat backup.

What the camera sees and what it cannot

The camera head rides on a flexible push cable with a light and a distance counter. It travels from a cleanout or a pulled toilet toward the street, showing pipe material, joints, roots, grease buildup, standing water and breaks.

The counter tells you how far down the line each fault sits. A locator can then mark that spot above ground, which saves digging in the wrong place.

The camera does not show what is behind the pipe wall, and it cannot pass a full blockage. A line that is flooded must be cleared first, which is why many inspections follow a drain cleaning visit.

Buying a house: what the report should answer

A home inspection rarely looks inside the sewer line. A camera report fills that gap, and these are the questions it should answer before you close.

  • What is the pipe made of, and is it consistent from the house to the street or connection point?
  • Are joints tight, or are there offsets, roots or cracks that will become repairs?
  • Is there a belly, a low spot that holds water and waste?
  • Is the cleanout in the right place, and does it open freely?
  • How far from the house does each fault sit, and is it under paving, a tree or a garden?

Ten minutes inside a new pipe, an hour inside an old one

Hopkinton grew faster than any other community in Greater Boston between the 2010 and 2020 censuses, and Westborough has seen steady building too. Newer lines are mostly plastic and should show clean joints, but builders can leave debris and poorly seated joints inside.

A camera survey on a recent build can confirm the work before warranty periods end. Older lines take longer to inspect, since the plumber stops at each fault to record distance and condition. The Hopkinton emergency plumbing page explains how the same inspection is offered across the town line.

Old pipe in a town with mill-era streets

Westborough's shoe, boot and straw hat factories date to the 1800s, and the Vintonville Historic District and West Main Street Historic District reflect that era. Homes and former commercial buildings of that age may have iron or clay sewer pipe that was never replaced.

A camera survey is the cheapest way to learn that before you buy or renovate. If the footage shows iron with heavy corrosion, you can plan a replacement on your schedule instead of after a backup.

Using the recording after the visit

The recording is yours. Keep a copy with your closing documents or your home maintenance file. If you sell, a recent clean recording tells a buyer the line was checked.

If the footage shows damage, you can use it to compare quotes from other contractors, since all of them can see the same fault at the same distance. Pair it with sewer line repair planning when the findings call for it.

When an inspection is not enough

A camera is a diagnostic tool, not a repair. If the pipe is flooded, the inspection waits until the line is cleared. If the findings are borderline, the plumber may recommend a second look after cleaning.

Inspections also do not replace a septic system evaluation. If your home is on septic, ask who should assess the tank and the field, since that is typically a different specialist.
