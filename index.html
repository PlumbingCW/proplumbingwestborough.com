Plumbing emergencies we dispatch for around the clock

An emergency plumber is worth calling when water, sewage or heat is failing and waiting will cost more than the call. In Westborough that usually means one of the situations below, and every one of them is dispatched 24/7, including nights, weekends and holidays.

If you smell gas, leave the building first and call the gas utility or 911 from outside. A plumber cannot make that situation safe over the phone, so the gas company comes before us.

  • A burst or split pipe pushing water into a wall, ceiling or basement. Close the main shutoff valve, then call.
  • A sewer line backup bringing wastewater up through floor drains, tubs or toilets.
  • A water heater leaking from the tank, or no hot water on a winter night.
  • A boiler that stopped heating the house during a cold snap.
  • A sump pump that failed while groundwater is rising in the basement.
  • A toilet or supply line overflowing and not stopping at the shutoff.

What Westborough homes ask of their plumbing

Westborough's public water comes from nine groundwater wells plus the Westboro Reservoir, also known as Sandra Pond, according to the state's source water assessment. The town adds fluoride, uses potassium hydroxide and zinc orthophosphate for corrosion control, and disinfects with chlorine.

That assessment is older, so read the current annual water quality report from the Westborough Water Department before you buy a filter. Corrosion control exists to protect pipes, so a color change at one tap alone can point to the plumbing at that tap and deserves a look.

Houses inside the Vintonville and West Main Street historic districts are older by definition, because the districts are listed for their historic buildings. Older houses are more likely to have galvanized steel supply lines, cast iron drains and repairs made with mixed materials. When one of those pipes leaks, it is worth having the pipe around it inspected, and the repiping page is the one to read if the same house keeps springing leaks.

The town sits where I-90 and I-495 meet, with Route 9 carrying most commercial traffic and Route 30 meeting Route 135 at the rotary in the center. That layout matters on a rush hour call, because traffic on those roads shapes how quickly a plumber reaches each side of town. It is one reason we ask for the exact address and the nearest cross street when you call.

Lake Chauncy, Sandra Pond and Hocomonco Pond all sit within town limits. Homes near water often deal with high groundwater in spring, and that is a common reason for a sump pump to run constantly and then fail. Test the pump with a bucket of water before the thaw, and call early if it struggles.

Plumbing in Massachusetts falls under 248 CMR 10.00, the Uniform State Plumbing Code, and gas fitting under 248 CMR 4.00. Permit rules vary, so ask the Westborough Building Department at Town Hall before a larger project. You can also check any plumber's license with the Massachusetts Board of State Examiners of Plumbers and Gas Fitters.

Emergency plumbing near Westborough: five neighboring towns

Dispatch covers Westborough and the towns around it. Northborough sits just to the north and draws its water from the MWRA system, so homes there share a supply that is monitored for lead and copper. Southborough lies to the east along Route 9 and also buys all of its water from the MWRA.

Shrewsbury to the west runs on municipal wells treated for manganese, which matters if you notice staining on fixtures. Grafton to the southwest has three villages with three ZIP codes, and Hopkinton to the southeast is in Middlesex County, where roughly four in ten residents rely on private wells that the town does not treat.

The ZIP codes we dispatch to are 01581 for Westborough, 01532 for Northborough, 01772 for Southborough, 01545 for Shrewsbury, 01519, 01536 and 01560 for Grafton, and 01748 for Hopkinton. The service area page shows how the towns fit together on one map.

What a clear price before work starts actually means

The cost of a plumbing repair depends on what the plumber finds, so a number quoted before anyone sees the problem is a guess. Our plumber looks at the problem first, explains the options in plain language, and gives you the price before work starts.

The main drivers are access, parts and time. A shutoff valve in an open basement is a short job, while the same repair behind a finished wall takes longer. After-hours calls carry different labor rates than scheduled work, and the plumber tells you that up front instead of adding it later.

Licensed and insured means the plumber holds a Massachusetts license and the work is covered if something goes wrong. If you want to confirm a license, the state offers a check-a-license tool on mass.gov.
