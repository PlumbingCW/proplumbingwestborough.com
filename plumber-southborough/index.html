Read the house before you read the leak

A plumber walking into a burst-pipe call looks at the era of the house before touching a tool. An older home may have galvanized steel supply lines that rust from the inside and choke pressure. A newer one may have PEX that fails at a fitting instead of along a pipe.

That is why the right first question on the phone is the build year. It tells us which parts to load, how many hours the repair may take and whether a repiping conversation belongs in the visit.

Southborough's three villages, Cordaville, Fayville and Southville, sit along the same town roads as newer streets, so one address can differ from its neighbor by several decades of plumbing practice.

Signs the plumbing is older than the paint

Fresh paint and new cabinets hide the pipes behind them. These clues point to the original plumbing.

  • Water pressure that falls when a second fixture opens, a pattern common with corroded galvanized supply lines.
  • Rust-brown water for a few seconds after the house has been unused overnight.
  • Cast iron drain pipes in the basement with flaking scale or rust streaks at the joints.
  • Greenish crust or small pinholes with white mineral spray on copper lines.
  • A basement floor drain that backs up in wet weather and clears on its own.
  • A water heater tank older than 12 years with rust at the fittings.

Getting to Southborough without a highway ramp

I-495 and the Massachusetts Turnpike pass through Southborough but neither has an interchange within the town limits. Dispatch from Westborough therefore runs along Route 9 or Route 30, the two roads that cross town east to west, and Route 85 carries traffic north and south.

In practice that means we want the nearest main road when you describe where you live. An address off Route 85 reads differently than one in Fayville or Cordaville, and a precise cross street saves minutes at the end of the drive.

The MWRA supply and the vinyl-lined mains

Southborough buys 100 percent of its water from the MWRA under public water supply number 2277000. The town switched from the Hultman Aqueduct to the MetroWest Water Supply Tunnel in 2003.

State rules require the town to sample for PCE, a solvent, five times a year in areas with vinyl-lined asbestos-cement mains. The limit is 5 ppb, and the results listed ranged from not detected to 3.2 ppb. That sampling concerns the town's mains, not the pipes in your house.

What it means for you is simple. Questions about water quality go to the town and the MWRA reports. Questions about pressure, rust, leaks and fixtures belong to a plumber, and we can tell you whether a symptom comes from your pipe or points back to the street.

Campus-scale buildings and big old houses

St. Mark's School and the Fay School give the town a different kind of building stock from a street of colonials. Any building with many fixtures, long branch lines and rooms that sit empty for weeks poses its own risk, because water that stands in a rarely used line goes stale and a valve that is never exercised seizes.

Our commercial plumbing page explains how we handle multi-fixture buildings. For a large private house with a seldom-used wing, the same idea applies: run the lines before guests arrive and check every shutoff.

Low ground near the Sudbury Reservoir

The Sudbury Reservoir covers about one-tenth of the town's area. Land near a big body of water often keeps groundwater high in wet months, so a basement on low ground may see more work from its sump pump than one on a rise.

A pump has to start when the pit fills and a check valve has to keep water from flowing back. Our sump pump page covers testing and replacement, and a quick bucket test in the spring costs nothing.

Cast iron lines and the sewer lateral

Houses with original cast iron drains can develop scale inside the pipe that catches grease and paper. A camera inspection shows whether a recurring clog is pipe wear or a partial collapse, and sewer line repair covers spot repairs and lining.

A drain clearing that works for three weeks and fails again is a signal that something is physically wrong in the line. Ask for a camera look before paying for a fourth cabling.

If you have a burst pipe or active leak in Southborough

These steps are the same for every house, with one extra for older homes.

Shut the main valve

In an older house the valve may be stiff from years of rest. Turn it slowly and never force it with a pipe wrench or you may snap the stem.

Kill breakers to wet rooms

Switch off power to rooms with standing water or a wet ceiling before you step into them.

Clear the area

Lift rugs, move furniture and open the cabinet doors under the sink to let air reach the wet surfaces.

Call the 24/7 line

Tell dispatch the approximate year the house was built and the pipe you can see, because it changes which parts we bring.
