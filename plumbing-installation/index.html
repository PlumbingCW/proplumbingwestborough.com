What counts as a plumbing installation

Installation covers any new work rather than repair of something that failed. That includes a new bathroom, a kitchen remodel, a laundry hookup, an outdoor spigot, a basement bathroom, a wet bar and replacement fixtures such as a vanity, toilet or shower valve. It also covers new appliances like dishwashers and water treatment equipment.

Each of these touches the same three systems: supply, drain and vent. Supply brings pressure to the fixture. Drain moves wastewater away by gravity at the right slope. Vent lets air enter so drains flow smoothly and sewer gas stays outside. A good plan checks all three before a pipe is cut.

From rough-in to final: the phases of a new-build job

New construction follows a sequence. During rough-in, plumbers run supply lines, drains and vents through open framing before walls close. An inspection usually happens at that stage, so the rough-in must be accessible. Later comes the finish stage, when fixtures, faucets and appliances are set and tested.

The sequence matters because mistakes are cheap before drywall and costly after. Westborough has seen steady growth in households, 7,930 at the 2020 count, and that means builders and homeowners work with the town on schedules. Gas appliance work: leave the building first

Gas appliances and lines need a stricter rule than water. If you smell gas, which is often described as rotten eggs, do not search for the source. Leave the building at once and take everyone, including pets. Do not switch lights on or off, do not use a phone inside, and do not start a car in an attached garage.

From outside, call the gas utility or 911 first, before you call a plumber. Do not go back in until responders say it is safe. After the gas company clears the building, a licensed gas fitter can inspect and repair the line. We never promise a response time for a gas leak, because the utility and fire department come first.

  • Leave the building immediately with everyone inside.
  • Do not touch switches, lighters, phones or garage door openers on the way out.
  • Call the gas utility or 911 from a safe distance.
  • Stay out until professionals clear the building.
  • Then call a licensed gas fitter for the repair.

Gas lines and licensing in Massachusetts

New gas piping, range hookups, dryer lines and gas fireplaces fall under the Massachusetts fuel gas code, 248 CMR 4.00, with amendments to NFPA 54 in 248 CMR 5.00. Work must be done by a licensed gas fitter, and permits and inspections apply. Ask the Westborough Building Department how they handle gas permits.

You can confirm a license yourself. The Board of State Examiners of Plumbers and Gas Fitters licenses journeymen and masters, and the state Check a license tool on mass.gov lets you look up a name. Hiring unlicensed help risks failed inspections and insurance problems.

Remodels: kitchens, bathrooms and basements

A remodel is the best time to fix plumbing you have been tolerating, because walls and floors are already open. Before the first demolition day, settle the following items with your plumber so the schedule holds.

  • Final fixture locations, since moving a toilet or shower drain changes the framing.
  • Whether the supply lines in the walls will be reused or replaced.
  • Where shutoff valves will sit, and that each fixture has its own.
  • Venting for any new sink, especially a kitchen island.
  • Water heater capacity for the added fixtures.
  • Who pulls permits and who schedules inspections.

New construction near the Turnpike and Route 9 corridor

Growth around the I-90 and I-495 junction and along Route 9 has brought new homes, additions and commercial work. New buildings need properly sized supply and drain pipes, a plan for vents and a sound connection to the street. Connections to the water main or sewer follow the utility's rules, so ask the Westborough Water Department.

We coordinate rough-in with framers and electricians so pipes do not collide with ducts or wiring. For sewers and drains on larger jobs, see our work on sewer line repair, which also covers line replacement, and repiping covers new supply lines inside the house.

A note on water treatment equipment

Customers often ask about softeners and filters during an installation. We will not guess at your water quality, and we do not state a hardness figure for Westborough because the right number is on your utility report. Test your tap with a home kit or a certified lab, and ask the Westborough Water Department for its current annual water quality report.

Other towns have their own issues. Hopkinton reported iron, manganese and PFAS concerns in recent years, and about 40 percent of residents there rely on private wells, which need testing by a certified lab. In Shrewsbury, the town treats active wells for manganese according to an older MassDEP assessment. Our Shrewsbury emergency plumber page has more.

Things to ready before the plumber arrives

A little preparation keeps an install on schedule and saves you labor charges.

  • Clear access to the work area, including the path from the door.
  • Fixtures, faucets and appliances on site and unboxed to check for damage.
  • Manufacturer instructions and model numbers for specialty equipment.
  • The location of your main shutoff and electrical panel.
  • A decision on who removes and disposes of old fixtures.
