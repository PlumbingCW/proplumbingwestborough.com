Winter: frozen lines and the burst pipe at 3 a.m.

Pipes freeze where cold air meets water: an exterior wall, a crawlspace, a garage ceiling or a hose bib that was never drained. Ice grows inside the pipe, pressure builds between the ice and a closed faucet, and the pipe splits. The leak often starts only when the ice melts.

If a faucet runs slowly or not at all on a hard cold night, treat it as a frozen pipe and open the tap. Warm the pipe with moving air. Never use an open flame. Our burst pipe repair page covers the repair routes once a pipe has split.

Before the first hard freeze

A half hour in October removes most winter emergencies.

  • Disconnect garden hoses and close the interior shutoff to each outdoor spigot, then open the spigot to drain it.
  • Insulate pipes along exterior walls, in crawlspaces and above garage ceilings.
  • Seal drafts around pipe penetrations in rim joists and sill plates.
  • Know where your main shutoff is and make sure it turns.
  • Leave the heat on at a minimum setting if you travel, and let a cabinet door stay open on a cold night.
  • Test the sump pump by lifting the float so you know it runs before the thaw.

Thaw and spring: sumps, sewers and groundwater

Snowmelt and spring rain soak the ground, and a basement on low ground may see water around the foundation. A sump pump that starts easily in March and then fails during the third day of rain is a regular spring call.

Spring is also when tree roots find a cracked sewer lateral and when a heavy storm can overload a floor drain. If a power outage knocks the pump out during a storm, a battery backup keeps it running. Shrewsbury also remembers the 1953 Worcester tornado, which killed twelve people in the area, and storm power loss is a good reason to think about a backup before the next one.

Summer: irrigation, lake season and the meter

Sprinkler zones and hose use push the meter up in summer, which makes a buried leak harder to spot. Shut the irrigation system off, read the meter, wait 15 minutes and read it again. If the dial moves, the leak is in the house line or a buried pipe and not the lawn.

Lake Quinsigamond and parks like Dean Park and Prospect Park bring crowds and guests to town. A full house puts fixtures under heavy use, and a toilet that was marginal in February becomes a weekend emergency. See toilet repair for the usual fixes.

Fall: the cold morning that finds the old water heater

Heaters coast through summer and fail when the incoming water turns cold and demand rises. A unit older than 12 years with rust at the fittings or a puddle at its base should be planned for replacement before it is forced.

The water heater replacement page explains sizing and venting. Booking a swap in October is calmer than finding an empty tank at 6 a.m. in January.

The wells behind the tap

Shrewsbury Water Department draws from municipal groundwater wells, including the South Street, Sewell Street, Lamberts Sand Pit and Home Farm wells. MassDEP's assessment says the active wells in the Sewell Street and Lamberts area are treated for manganese removal, disinfected with chlorine and fluoridated.

The same assessment, an older one, rates the aquifer highly vulnerable because it lacks clay barriers and gives the system a high susceptibility rating. That is a screening rating and not a water quality result. For current numbers, read the department's latest annual report.

Practical result: if you see dark staining on fixtures or sudden cloudy water, tell the water department first and then call us if the problem stays inside your plumbing.

If a pipe has burst or water is rising in Shrewsbury

Winter and spring emergencies call for these four steps.

Close the main valve

In a freeze, open the nearest faucet afterward to let the thawed water drain instead of flooding the floor.

Cut power to the wet area

In spring flooding, do not wade into a basement where water touches outlets or the electrical panel. Call the utility if the panel is wet.

Lift valuables off the floor

Move boxes, rugs and electronics upstairs and open cabinet doors to dry the cavity.
