Downtown and West Main Street Historic District · Westborough, MA

Plumber in Downtown Westborough, MA

Downtown Westborough is the historic center of town, where Route 30 (Main Street) and Route 135 (South and Milk Streets) meet at the rotary. The West Main Street Historic District covers a large residential part of that center and part of its commercial center.

Whether the call is for a house, a rental or a small shop, call (508) 389-4045, give the address and the problem, and ask what service is available.

Downtown Westborough at a glance

The center

Route 30 (Main Street) and Route 135 (South and Milk Streets) meet at the rotary in the town center.

The historic district

Roughly bounded by Milk, Main, Blake and Fay Streets, about 45 acres, listed on the National Register in 1987.

Expanded since

Boundary increases were added in 1990, 2006 and 2009, taking in more of West Main Street and nearby streets.

Building styles

The listing names Greek Revival and Late Victorian architecture.

A district that mixes homes and businesses

The National Register listing describes a large residential area of the historic center together with part of the commercial center. That mix matters for plumbing, because one street can hold a family home, a rented apartment and a small business.

The district grew in stages. The 1987 listing covered West Main Street from its junction with Milk and South Streets west to Blake Street. Later increases reached further west, south toward Charles and Ruggles Streets, and north of Milk Street.

We describe the area by those anchor streets and not by a hard boundary line, because we have not found a map we can treat as exact. If your address is near the edge, the street name is the better guide.

Who calls from the center of town

The questions differ depending on who owns the building and who uses it.

Homeowners

Older houses and newer renovations sit side by side. Start with the basics: a main shutoff that works, a known meter location and a clear idea of where the water heater is. Water heater installation and drain cleaning are the usual first calls.

Renters and landlords

If you rent, the lease and the landlord decide who calls a plumber, so tell the landlord first. Landlords who need a repair arranged can call (508) 389-4045 and describe the building and the problem.

Small businesses

A shop, office or restaurant has different needs from a house, such as restrooms, floor drains and kitchen lines. Read about commercial plumbing and tell us what kind of business it is when you call.

A short list for mixed-use buildings

Gather these before the visit and the work goes faster.

  • Which shutoff serves your space, and where is it?
  • Does the problem affect another unit or only yours?
  • Who owns the building, and who must approve the repair?
  • For a business, where are the floor drains and cleanouts?
  • Has anyone noticed a damp ceiling or wall near the problem?

Older buildings, permits and the town

Permit rules vary by job, so ask the Westborough Building Department at Town Hall, 34 West Main Street, before work starts. Food businesses and some other uses can have extra plumbing rules, and the town is the place to ask.

Plumbing work in Massachusetts falls under 248 CMR 10.00. Where a building is in a historic district, ask the town whether any review applies to exterior changes, since that is separate from plumbing rules.

Before you call from downtown

Five minutes of preparation saves time on site.

Shut off what you control

Close the fixture valve or your unit's shutoff. Do not force a stuck valve.

Tell the building owner

In a rental or shared building, let the owner or manager know early.

Protect the space

Move stock, papers and electronics away from wet areas and put a bucket under a drip.

Give the address, the type of building and the problem when you call (508) 389-4045.

Code and standards note. Plumbing in Westborough follows 248 CMR 10.00, the Uniform State Plumbing Code. The Massachusetts Board of State Examiners of Plumbers and Gas Fitters licenses plumbers, and homeowners can look up any license with the state's Check a license tool on mass.gov.
