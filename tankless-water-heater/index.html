What a tankless heater changes about your plumbing

A tankless unit heats water as it flows, so there is no tank to rust through and no standby loss. The trade is a much bigger burner or element, which makes the gas line, venting and electrical capacity decisions bigger than they are for a tank.

Flow is the other limit. Every unit has a maximum rise in temperature at a given flow rate, and a cold January inlet temperature cuts the gallons per minute it can deliver. Sizing for two showers at once matters more than the brand.

Flushing and descaling: the service most units skip

Minerals in the water slowly coat the inside of the heat exchanger. The coating insulates the metal, raises burner temperatures and eventually triggers error codes or a cold water sandwich. Descaling dissolves it using a mild acid solution circulated through the unit with a small pump.

Most manufacturers call for a flush about once a year, with shorter intervals where water is mineral rich. We use isolation valves so the service is a quick job, and we install them if your unit lacks them. A unit flushed on schedule runs quieter and keeps its efficiency.

Ask us to inspect the inlet screen and the condensate trap on condensing models. A clogged trap shuts a unit down, and it takes five minutes to clear.

Installing tankless in an older Massachusetts house

Retrofits are the main challenge. Many older houses have half-inch gas lines that cannot feed a tankless burner, and flue paths that were built for a draft hood, not a sidewall vent. Replacing the line and the vent are often the largest parts of the budget.

We measure the gas run, count the appliances and calculate the load before recommending a model. If the numbers do not work, we tell you and suggest a high-efficiency tank. The water heater installation page compares both paths.

Condensing, non-condensing and electric units

Condensing models extract more heat from the exhaust, so they vent through PVC or polypropylene and drain condensate that needs a neutralizer. Non-condensing units use metal vent pipe and ship with simpler plumbing.

Electric tankless heaters have no flue at all, but they draw serious current. A whole-house electric unit can demand a larger service panel, so the electrical review comes first. Point-of-use electric units under a sink are a different, smaller tool.

Maintenance checklist for tankless owners

A short routine extends the life of the unit and avoids most service calls.

  • Flush and descale annually, or sooner if you see fluctuating temperatures.
  • Clean the inlet water screen each time you flush.
  • Check the condensate trap and neutralizer on condensing units.
  • Keep the area around the unit clear and the vent terminal free of snow and debris.
  • Test the carbon monoxide alarm nearest the unit each month.
  • Note any new error code and the date it appears, since repeats point to a root cause.

Repair or replace a tankless unit

Tankless heaters are built for longer service than tanks, with typical life of up to twenty years when maintained. Once a heat exchanger leaks or a main control board fails on an old unit, the repair math can favor replacement.

If your unit is down and you need hot water today, ask about a temporary approach. Our team also handles emergency work through water heater repair, including units of other types.
