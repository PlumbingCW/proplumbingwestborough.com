When water is backing up or draining slowly

Slow drains and backups are the most common calls, and the cause decides the fix. A single sink that gurgles usually has a local clog, and drain cleaning clears it with a snake or an auger. When several fixtures slow down together, the blockage is in the main line, and hydro jetting scours the pipe wall with high-pressure water instead of punching a hole through the clog.

If a sewer backup keeps returning, the pipe itself may be damaged. A sewer camera inspection shows roots, bellies and cracks before anyone digs. After that, sewer line cleaning handles buildup, and sewer line repair covers broken or collapsed pipe, including lining that avoids a trench.

Hot water and heat that stopped working

A tank that leaks from the bottom is usually finished, while no hot water with no leak is often a failed element, thermostat or pilot. Water heater repair fixes the second group, and water heater replacement takes over when the tank has failed. Planning ahead is cheaper than waiting for the failure, and water heater installation covers new locations, venting and fuel changes.

If you want endless hot water and lower standby loss, tankless water heaters are installed, descaled and repaired as their own service. In a New England winter, a boiler that stops heating is an emergency, and boiler repair is dispatched like any other after-hours call.

Leaks, bursts and pipes that need replacing

Water you can see is easy to stop. Water you cannot see is how bills climb and walls rot, and leak detection uses acoustic and thermal tools to find the source without opening every wall. When a pipe has already split or frozen, burst pipe repair is the emergency page to read.

The line that brings water from the street can fail on its own. Water line repair covers that buried pipe, and repiping replaces tired galvanized or failing supply lines throughout a house. New construction and remodels fall under plumbing installation.

Fixtures, disposals and the basement

Small fixture failures cause outsized damage when they are ignored. A toilet that runs or leaks at the base needs toilet repair, dripping handles and failed cartridges go to faucet repair, and a jammed or leaking garbage disposal is usually a repair, not a replacement.

Below grade, Westborough's spring groundwater and heavy storms put basements to work. A sump pump is the first defense, and basement waterproofing addresses seepage that a pump alone cannot stop.

Plumbing for businesses

Restaurants, offices and retail along Route 9 and Route 30 need plumbing done outside business hours. Commercial plumbing covers after-hours repairs, grease handling, backflow testing and leak work for property managers, with scheduling that keeps the business open.

Emergency or scheduled: how to decide

Treat it as an emergency when water is moving where it should not, sewage is coming up, heat is out in freezing weather, or a pump has failed with water rising. Those situations get worse by the hour, and dispatch answers 24/7.

Treat it as scheduled when the fixture still works but is aging, such as a water heater near the end of its typical service life or a toilet that runs now and then. Planned jobs are easier on your terms, and same-day appointments are available when the schedule allows.

What every service call includes

The details change by service, but the structure of the visit does not.

  • A licensed plumber diagnoses the problem at your property before any work begins.
  • A clear price for the repair, explained before work starts.
  • The repair itself, using parts matched to your fixtures and to the Massachusetts plumbing code.
  • A plain explanation of what failed and how to avoid a repeat.

Neighborhoods in Westborough and what they tend to need

Older housing near the Vintonville and West Main Street historic districts tends to raise drain, supply line and repiping questions. Newer subdivisions and the commercial strips along Route 9 and Route 30 tend to raise water heater, fixture and after-hours commercial calls. Homes near Lake Chauncy, Sandra Pond and Hocomonco Pond tend to see basement water in spring.

None of that is a rule, but it helps you pick the page that most likely matches your problem.
