North Grafton: the rail stop, the campus and the old hospital grounds

North Grafton has a station on the MBTA Framingham/Worcester Line and a freight line, the Grafton and Upton Railroad, that runs to Milford. It also holds the Tufts Cummings School of Veterinary Medicine and the grounds where Grafton State Hospital operated from 1901 to 1973.

Commuter households leave the house empty for ten hours or more, so a small leak can run all day before anyone notices. A water shutoff or a leak sensor on the heater helps, and a meter test finds the rest. Large institutional buildings fall under our commercial plumbing page.

Grafton village: the Common and the older center

Grafton Common, the Willard House and Clock Museum and the Hassanamisco Nipmuc reservation anchor the center of town. The Nipmuc village here dates to 1647, and the land was divided in 1727 before the town was incorporated in 1735.

That long history means the center holds older buildings. Houses of that kind may have galvanized steel supply lines and cast iron drains, so repiping and drain work come up often. Replacing a pipe before it fails costs less than repairing a ceiling afterward.

South Grafton and the Blackstone River mill streets

South Grafton, Farnumsville, Fisherville, Saundersville and Axtell Corner grew up along the Blackstone River in the industrial era, and Tupperware was founded in South Grafton in 1938. Villages with industrial-era roots often have close-set houses and older sewer connections.

Old laterals crack and fill with roots. A camera inspection tells us whether a recurring backup is a root mass or a failed section, and our sewer line repair page shows the options from spot repair to lining.

Signs a mill-village house needs more than a quick fix

One of these alone is a warning. Several together mean a larger plan.

  • The kitchen sink clears with a cable but backs up again within a month.
  • Water pressure drops sharply when the shower and a second fixture run together.
  • Brown water appears after the house has been unused for a day.
  • Cast iron in the basement shows scale, rust bleeding or a rough interior at cleanouts.
  • Floor drains gurgle when the washer drains.
  • Pinhole leaks have appeared in two separate places in copper lines.

Water: ask the district, not the plumber

A Grafton Water District filing with MassDEP describes a proposed groundwater well near Lake Ripple behind the high school, with about 0.86 million gallons a day of withdrawal. The filing is undated and a proposal, so it does not tell you what flows in your tap today.

For present sources, treatment and results, ask the Grafton Water District for its current annual water quality report. A plumber handles pressure, fixtures, heaters and leaks, and we are happy to say which part of a symptom looks like a supply issue and which sits inside the house.
