Water Meter Leak Test: How to Find a Hidden Leak in Westborough, MA

Your water meter keeps a running count of every gallon that enters the house. If that count rises while nothing is using water, you have a leak somewhere between the meter and the fixtures.

The test below takes about twenty minutes. It does not find the leak, but it answers the first question: is there one, and is it likely inside or outside the house?

Before you start: who owns the meter

The meter belongs to the water utility, not to you. In Westborough that is the Westborough Water Department. Do not remove, tamper with or adjust the meter. Lifting the lid and reading the dial is fine. Anything beyond that is a call to the department.

If the meter is in a pit outside, stay clear of traffic and watch for insects and standing water. If it is inside, usually in a basement near the front wall, it is easier to reach.

Step by step: the twenty minute test

Run the steps in order and write the numbers down.

  • Turn off every faucet, appliance and irrigation system.
  • Find the meter and wipe the cover of the read-out window.
  • Look for a small triangle, star or flow indicator that spins when any water moves.
  • Write down the full reading, including the small dial digits.
  • Wait twenty minutes without using any water.
  • Read the meter again and compare.

Reading the result

Any change in the numbers means water moved. The size and pattern of the change tells you what to try next.

Narrowing it down: inside or outside the house

Close the main shutoff valve where the line enters the house. Watch the meter for ten minutes. If the indicator stops, the leak is on the house side of the valve. If it keeps moving, the leak is on the service line between the meter and the valve, or the valve itself is leaking.

A leak on the service line is a different repair from a leak in a wall. It involves excavation, and that is where a licensed plumber and a locate of the underground line matter.

The five-minute checks for the most common culprits

Run these after the test shows a leak inside the house.

  • Drop food coloring in each toilet tank, wait ten minutes and look for color in the bowl.
  • Look under every sink and around the dishwasher and washing machine hoses.
  • Check the water heater drain valve and the relief valve discharge pipe.
  • Walk outside and check hose bibs and any irrigation valves.
  • Listen quietly at night for faint hissing near walls and floors.

The sneaky ones: toilets and irrigation

A toilet flapper that does not seal can lose a startling amount of water without making noise. The dye test catches it every time. A sprinkler valve stuck slightly open does the same underground, and you may only notice a soggy patch of lawn.

If the meter moves slowly and the toilets are clean, shut off the irrigation system at its own valve and test again.

When to call for help

Call right away if water is visible in a ceiling, if the floor is warm in one spot, or if the meter spins fast with the main valve open. Shut the main valve first and keep it closed until a plumber arrives.

For a high bill with no visible cause, record the readings from your test and call. Those numbers shorten the diagnostic visit and let the technician plan which tools to bring.

Keep the numbers

Write down the date, time, both readings and what you shut off. If the leak turns out to be on the utility side, those notes support a conversation with the water department. If it is on your side, they help an insurance adjuster understand when the leak started.

Water meter leak test: questions answered

It is a reliable yes-or-no test for a leak downstream of the meter. It cannot tell you where the leak is, but it tells you whether to keep looking and whether the problem is inside or outside the house.

If every fixture is off and the indicator turns even slowly, water is moving. Slow movement often means a toilet flapper or a dripping fixture, but a small underground leak can look the same. Repeat the test after the dye check.

Use the main shutoff valve in the house instead. The curb stop and the meter belong to the utility, and operating them without permission can cause problems.

Billing adjustments are a decision for the water department. Keep your test notes and the plumber's report, and ask the department about its process.

Call the Westborough Water Department for meter, billing and utility-side questions. Call a licensed plumber when the leak turns out to be inside the house or on the pipe between the main valve and the meter.
