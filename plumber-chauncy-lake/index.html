Chauncy Lake at a glance

Where it is

The former Westborough State Hospital site on Lyman Street, at the Northborough town line. The state closed the hospital in 2010.

What it is

A community for people 55 and over. The builder lists single-level condominium homes of 958 to 1,512 square feet.

Features

The builder lists in-unit washers and dryers, underground parking in some homes, a clubhouse, a pool and walking trails.

Still growing

The builder's page shows homes with an expected completion in December 2026, so the community is still being completed.

Where Chauncy Lake sits, and what it is not

The community was built on 37 acres that the town sold when the old state hospital campus was redeveloped. Reports during planning described 700 units in 14 buildings. Because homes are still being completed, we do not state a unit count today.

Do not confuse the community with Lake Chauncy itself. The lake is a separate place, with a public beach open to residents of Westborough and Northborough in summer. The community takes its name from the lake and sits on Lyman Street.

Westborough State Hospital has its own National Register listing, along Lyman Street north of the lake. That listing is a separate matter from the new homes, and we mention it only so you can place the location.

Condo living: who handles what

Your condominium documents decide where the association's responsibility ends and yours begins. Read them before a problem happens, not during one.

In general, the first questions are the same in any condominium. Is the pipe inside your unit or in a shared wall? Does the association manage a common water line or a shared water heater? Who must be told before a plumber enters a building? The association or management company answers those, and a plumber cannot decide them for you.

In-unit laundry and the lines behind it

The builder lists in-unit washers and dryers. A washer is connected by two supply hoses that stay under pressure whenever the valves behind it are open. They are small parts, but they are a common source of sudden water in a home.

Check the hoses now and then for bulges, cracks or damp spots. Many owners close the washer valves when they travel for more than a few days. If you ever find water near the laundry area, start with leak detection and shut the unit's water off.

When a leak could reach a neighbor

Reports describe the community as multi-story buildings with single-level homes. In a multi-story building, water from one home can travel to the home below. That is why the first steps are to stop the water you control and to tell management right away.

Common jobs here are the same as in any condominium: water heater repair, toilet repair, faucet repair and drain cleaning. Ask the association whether there are contractor rules before anyone comes to the building.

A contact list worth keeping

Put these in one place so you can reach them quickly.

  • The association or management company's after-hours number.
  • Your building number, unit number and the street address.
  • The location of your unit's water shutoff.
  • The location of the water heater and a photo of its label.
  • A plumber you can call. Call (508) 389-4045 to ask what service is available.

If water is leaking in your home

Four steps limit the damage in a shared building.

Stop the water you control

Close the fixture valve or the unit's shutoff. Do not force a stuck valve.

Tell management at once

Water can reach other homes, so call the association or management company early.

Protect belongings

Move rugs, electronics and papers off wet floors and keep away from wet outlets.

Call with building details
