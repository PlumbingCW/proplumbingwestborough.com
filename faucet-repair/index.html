A valve that will not turn off: the first five minutes

When a faucet or supply line is spraying and the little valve under the sink will not close, the main shutoff is your only brake. It is normally near the water meter, often in the basement or utility area.

Turn the main clockwise slowly. Open one low faucet and one high faucet to drain the pressure. Then call us so we can replace the failed valve or line before you turn the water back on.

  • Close the fixture's stop valve first, and if it will not move, close the main shutoff.
  • Open a faucet on the lowest floor to bleed off the pressure.
  • Move anything electrical, rugs and cabinet contents out of the wet area.
  • Take a photo of the failed part before it is removed, for your records.
  • Do not force a corroded valve with a wrench, since the stem can snap and make the leak worse.

Kitchen and bath faucets: where the drip comes from

A faucet drips because something inside no longer seals. In a compression faucet it is the rubber washer or the brass seat. In a single-handle cartridge faucet it is the cartridge or its O-rings, and in a ball-style faucet it is the springs and seals.

Water that leaks around the base or the handle, rather than from the spout, points to worn O-rings or a loose packing nut. Water under the sink means the supply connections or the faucet body, and that is worth catching early because cabinets swell.

We replace the failing part when the faucet is otherwise sound. If the finish is pitted or the body is corroded, a new faucet is the better value, and we install the one you choose.

Shower valves and tub spouts

A shower that runs hot and cold is almost always the pressure-balance or thermostatic cartridge behind the handle. Replacing it means shutting the water to the bathroom and opening the trim, and the job is quick when parts are on the truck.

A tub spout that drips after the shower starts has a worn diverter. If the spout itself is loose against the wall, water can run behind the tile, so we check the seal and the wall around it before closing things up.

Persistent dampness around a shower valve is a leak inside the wall until proven otherwise. Our leak detection work uses moisture meters and thermal imaging to find it without opening the whole wall.

Tub and shower drains that back up

Most slow tubs are hair and soap held in the strainer or the first few inches of pipe. Before you call, try these in order.

  • Remove the stopper or strainer and pull out whatever has gathered.
  • Pour hot tap water, not boiling, down the drain to soften soap film.
  • Use a plastic drain zip tool rather than a chemical drain cleaner.
  • Check whether other fixtures gurgle, which suggests a deeper blockage.
  • Call us if the water rises in a second fixture, since the main branch line needs clearing.

What Westborough's water treatment means for your fixtures

MassDEP's source water assessment lists the Westborough Water Department's nine groundwater wells and one reservoir. It also notes that corrosion control is applied with potassium hydroxide and zinc orthophosphate. The report is older, so check the current annual water quality report for today's treatment.

No one can read hardness from a single spec sheet, so we do not guess it. The useful habit is to check aerators and showerheads twice a year. If grit or scale builds on them, tell your plumber, and ask the utility for its latest water quality numbers.

Stop valves, supply lines and the parts under the sink

Under every sink are two stop valves and two flexible supply lines. The braided lines are rated for years, not decades, and an old one can burst without warning when you are away. If you cannot remember replacing them, replace them.

Multi-turn gate valves in older homes often seize or leak when turned. We replace them with quarter-turn ball valves so that the next emergency has a shutoff that works. Where the wall is open or the cabinet allows, we also add a supply line to the dishwasher or ice maker if it is missing.
