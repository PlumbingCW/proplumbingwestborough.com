Westborough Village at a glance

Where it is

At the junction of Gleason Street and Smith Parkway, about half a mile from the commuter rail station, per a 2012 local report.

What was planned

A 276-unit project, with 33 units sold as affordable, described in 2012. Plans change, so treat that as the plan.

Home types

Townhomes, garden-style homes and single-family homes were in the plan. Five parks were planned.

When built

Resale listings describe townhomes built in 2015, with addresses such as Simeon Howard Way.

A community from the 2010s, not the 1800s

Westborough Village is the opposite of an old neighborhood. Listings describe townhomes built in 2015, which makes them roughly a decade old. Plumbing in a home that age is usually not the issue. Appliances and fixtures, on the other hand, start to age.

A tank water heater does not last forever, and a unit more than ten years old is worth having checked before it fails at an inconvenient hour. The same goes for fill valves, faucet cartridges and the hoses behind the washer.

We say this as general guidance. We have no record of any particular unit and we do not claim any problem is common in this community.

Owner or association: read your documents first

In a condominium, the master deed and the association's rules decide what is common property and what belongs to the owner. Read them to see where the association's responsibility ends and yours begins. Do not assume.

That matters when water is involved. A leak inside your unit is one situation. A leak in a shared wall or a common line is another, and the association may need to be told. Shut off the water you control, notify the association, and then call a plumber.

Plumbing jobs that suit a newer townhome

These are general jobs, listed by what owners tend to ask about.

Water heaters

If yours leaks or runs out of hot water fast, compare repair with replacement. Age and the cost of the fix both matter.

Leaks and water bills

A meter that moves with every fixture off signals a leak. Leak detection finds it without opening walls.

Fixtures

Dripping faucets and running toilets are small repairs. See faucet repair and toilet repair.

Drains

Slow kitchen or bath drains usually start with a clog in the branch line. See drain cleaning.

What to have ready before you call

A few facts help the dispatcher help you.

  • Your full address and unit number.
  • The name and number of your association or management company.
  • Where the unit's water shutoff is, and whether you have closed it.
  • A photo of the water heater label, which shows its age.
  • What changed just before the problem started.

If water is leaking in your unit

Four steps limit the damage.

Close the shutoff

Turn off the fixture valve or the unit's main shutoff. Do not force a stuck valve.

Tell the association

A leak in a shared building can reach a neighbor, so tell management early.

Keep people and electronics away

Move belongings off wet floors and stay clear of wet outlets.
