Jammed, humming or leaking: what to do right now

Never put a hand into a disposal, even when it is switched off at the wall. Turn the power off at the switch and the breaker, because a unit that restarts while you reach inside causes serious injuries.

If water is leaking from the unit, empty the cabinet under the sink, put a bucket below the leak and turn off the sink's hot and cold stop valves. If the dishwasher is also backing up, stop running it and call us.

  • Switch the disposal off at the wall switch, then at the breaker.
  • Do not use a chemical drain cleaner, which can burn skin and damage seals.
  • Look for the small red reset button on the underside of the unit.
  • Use an Allen key in the bottom hex socket only after the power is off.
  • Call us if the unit still hums or the cabinet is wet.

Why disposals hum, stall and trip

A humming disposal has power but the flywheel is stuck. A spoon, fruit pit, bottle cap or a ball of fibrous food waste is usually wedged between the impellers and the shredder ring. Turning the flywheel back and forth with the hex key frees it, and then the reset button restores power.

If the unit stays silent, the thermal overload has tripped or the electrical connection is loose. If it hums and trips again at once, the motor bearings may be failing, and replacement is the honest answer.

The dishwasher connection most people forget

The dishwasher drain hose connects to a small nipple on the side of the disposal. During installation, a plug inside that nipple must be knocked out, and if it is skipped, the dishwasher backs up into the sink.

We check that plug, the high loop in the hose and the air gap or loop that keeps sink water from siphoning back into the dishwasher. Both are quick checks that prevent a repeat visit.

Slow sink drain or standing water in the bowl

When the sink fills while the disposal runs, the problem is rarely the motor. A grease-coated trap or branch line behind the disposal is more common, and a plunger will not move it.

We clear the kitchen line from the trap with a small drain machine. Persistent clogs may point to a larger issue, and our drain cleaning service covers the camera and cable work for that case.

Replace or repair a disposal

A disposal has a limited life, and the choice comes down to a few practical points.

  • Repair when the problem is a jam, a loose gasket or a bad hose clamp.
  • Replace when the housing leaks, the bearings grind or the unit trips again quickly.
  • Choose horsepower by use: a half-horse unit suits light loads and a three-quarter to one horsepower unit handles larger households.
  • Check the cabinet for room, since larger units are taller.
  • Pick a continuous-feed model unless your household prefers batch feed.

Westborough kitchens and what goes down the drain

Westborough was incorporated in 1717 and has housing from many eras, so some kitchens still feed older drain lines. A disposal adds food solids to a line that an older house may have been built to carry only water and soap.

The practical rule is simple. Run cold water before, during and after use, and keep grease, coffee grounds, eggshell fragments and pasta out of the unit. Anything that expands or sets in the trap will clog a vintage branch line.
