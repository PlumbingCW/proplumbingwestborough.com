The tank has failed: what to do before anyone arrives

A split seam can release forty or fifty gallons faster than a floor drain can carry it. Acting in the first few minutes decides whether the damage stays on the concrete or reaches the walls.

  • Close the cold supply valve above the tank. If it will not turn, shut the main water valve where the line enters the house.
  • Cut power to an electric unit at the panel. For gas, turn the control knob off, and leave the building if you smell gas, then call the gas utility or 911.
  • Open one hot faucet upstairs to relieve pressure, and keep the hose bib closed until the plumber arrives.
  • Lift stored items off the floor, and keep children and pets out of standing water near outlets.
  • Photograph the water line, the tank label and the damage before you mop, since the images help any insurance claim.

Sizing a new tank for the household you have now

Most Westborough houses are served well by a forty or fifty gallon tank, and larger families or homes with a soaking tub can step up to sixty-five or eighty. The number to compare is the first-hour rating on the yellow EnergyGuide label, not the tank volume alone.

Bathrooms added since the heater was bought change the math. A household that moved from two people to four will outrun a tank that was fine for the last owners.

Fuel is the second decision. If the house already has a gas line and a flue, a gas replacement fits without new infrastructure. Switching fuel adds work, covered on our water heater installation page.

Expansion tanks and the pressure problem that kills new heaters

Water expands when heated. In a closed system with a backflow device or pressure reducing valve, that expansion has nowhere to go, so pressure climbs every time the heater fires. The result is a dripping relief valve and a shortened tank life.

A small expansion tank on the cold line absorbs the pressure swing. We test static pressure and tank pre-charge on each replacement, and add or replace the expansion tank when the system calls for it. A twelve-year-old expansion tank that has lost its air charge is a common hidden reason new tanks fail early.

What the replacement visit includes, in order

A same-day replacement is a defined sequence, and each step protects the house or the new tank.

  • Shut off water and fuel, drain the old tank to a floor drain or sump and disconnect.
  • Haul away the old unit so it does not sit in the garage for a month.
  • Install new flexible connectors, a new shutoff valve and dielectric fittings where dissimilar metals meet.
  • Pipe the temperature and pressure relief valve to a safe discharge point.
  • Check venting, combustion air and gas connections on a gas unit, and test for leaks.
  • Set the thermostat to a safe temperature, commonly 120 degrees Fahrenheit, and show you the shutoffs.

Heat pump options for a tank being replaced

Some callers ask about swapping a forty gallon gas tank for a heat pump water heater. It is a real option, and it changes the project. A heat pump unit needs electrical capacity, air space around it and a condensate drain, and it cools the room it sits in.

It is not right for every basement or closet, and the savings depend on your electric rates and your usage. We walk through the layout and the electrical panel before recommending it. Because incentives change from year to year, check the current state and utility programs before you buy rather than counting on any specific rebate.

The permit question

Water heater replacement is regulated plumbing work in Massachusetts, and gas appliances fall under the state fuel gas code, 248 CMR 4.00. Permit rules vary, so ask the Westborough Building Department what your project needs. We tell you in advance who pulls the permit and what the inspector will look at.

A licensed plumber matters here. You can confirm any license through the state Check a license tool on mass.gov, which draws on the Board of State Examiners of Plumbers and Gas Fitters. If the heater has been leaking for weeks, a leak detection visit can confirm no hidden line is wet.

What shapes the price of a new water heater

The quote is built from a short list of factors, and you see the full number before work starts.

Tank size and efficiencyA sixty-five gallon high-efficiency unit costs more than a basic forty gallon tank, and the label tells you why.
Fuel and ventingGas tanks need a safe flue, and a sidewall or power-vent system costs more than a standard draft hood.
Code itemsExpansion tanks, new shutoff valves, seismic-style strapping where required and discharge piping are priced as separate line items.
Access and haul-awayA tank in a finished basement or up a flight of stairs takes longer to move out and in.
Time of dayAfter-hours emergency installs follow a different labor rate than a booked weekday visit.

A sudden tank rupture is often covered for resulting water damage, though the tank itself rarely is. Gradual leaks and rust stains are usually excluded, so keep your photographs and the failed unit label.
