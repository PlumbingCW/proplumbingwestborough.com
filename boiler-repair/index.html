The boiler is off and the house is cooling: first steps

No heat in a Westborough winter is an emergency, especially with young children, older adults or exposed pipes. Work through these steps while you wait.

  • Check the thermostat, its batteries and the boiler switch near the stairs or the unit. Tripped breakers and closed zone valves are common.
  • Look at the pressure gauge. A reading near zero means a leak or a lost fill, and a reading near the relief valve rating means a stuck component.
  • If you smell gas, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 before calling a plumber. Do not relight anything.
  • If the boiler is leaking, switch it off and close the water feed valve, then catch drips with a bucket.
  • Open cabinets under sinks on exterior walls to keep supply pipes from freezing, and run a trickle of water in the coldest pipes.

Hot-water boilers and steam systems repair differently

Most modern residential boilers circulate hot water. Older homes may still use steam, where water in the boiler turns to vapor and rises through the pipes. Steam systems are sensitive to water level, vent condition and pipe pitch.

We confirm which type you have at the start of the visit. A hot-water pressure gauge and a steam sight glass tell very different stories, and treating one like the other creates new problems.

Checks we make before touching a part

A boiler call starts with measurement, not guesses.

  • Thermostat call for heat and low-voltage power at the control.
  • System pressure, expansion tank charge and relief valve condition.
  • Fuel supply, ignition sequence and flame signal.
  • Flue and combustion air, with a carbon monoxide reading at the vent.
  • Circulator pump amp draw and zone valve operation.
  • Visible leaks, rust trails and stained piping near the unit.

Radiators that heat unevenly

A boiler that runs fine while some rooms stay cold is usually a distribution problem. Trapped air, closed valves, a clogged baseboard or a failed zone valve each create cold spots. Bleeding air is the simplest fix, and a stuck zone valve is the most common part replacement.

If the system has not been serviced in years, a flush and inhibitor treatment can bring the output back. Persistent cold zones sometimes point to pipe damage, and a leak detection visit can find wet spots behind walls.
