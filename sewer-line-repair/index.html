Sewage in the house: what to do in the first ten minutes

Sewage backing into a tub, shower or floor drain is a health and property emergency. The water in the house is carrying waste, and it is rising from the lowest opening.

  • Stop all water use at once, including toilets, laundry and dishwashers.
  • Keep everyone out of the wet area, and keep pets away. Wear boots and gloves if you must enter.
  • If water is near an outlet, appliance or the furnace, leave the breaker alone unless you can reach it dry.
  • Open windows for air, but do not use the area as living space until it is cleaned.
  • Call with the location of the cleanout if you know it, and say whether neighbors have the same problem.

Cracked, offset or root-filled: reading the damage

A sewer lateral rarely fails in a dramatic way. It usually shows slow drains in the whole house, a gurgle in the toilet when the washer runs, or a damp patch in the yard. A camera confirms which of the common faults is present.

Root intrusion starts at a joint and widens it. An offset joint, where two pipes no longer line up, catches paper and waste. A crushed or collapsed section holds water in a belly and cannot be cleared by any tool.

Each of those has a different repair, and a stubborn clog that keeps returning after drain cleaning is often the first sign. Roots at one joint may need only a spot repair. A long belly or a crushed run usually needs replacement.

Trenchless lining and what it can fix

A liner is a resin-soaked sleeve that is pulled into the old pipe and cured in place. It forms a smooth new pipe inside the old one and seals cracks and joints, which also stops roots from entering.

Lining works when the host pipe is round and holds its shape. It cannot lift a belly or rejoin a pipe that has pulled apart. A camera survey beforehand is the only way to know, which is why sewer camera inspection comes before any decision.

Old iron under older Westborough lots

Westborough was incorporated in 1717, and parts of town were built up around the shoe and straw hat factories long before modern plastic pipe. Older homes in areas like the West Main Street and Vintonville historic districts may still have cast iron sewer pipe running from the house to the street.

Cast iron lasts for generations but corrodes from the inside and cracks under the weight of soil and frost. If your home is old, ask the plumber to check the iron section by camera, because replacing a short bad stretch can avoid a repair of the whole run.

Is it the sewer line or the septic system?

Not every home in this part of Worcester County is on a municipal sewer. Homes on a septic system have a tank and a leach field instead of a street connection, and the symptoms of failure overlap with a sewer line problem.

Slow drains, sewage odors and soggy ground over the field are septic clues. A plumber can tell you which system you have, but pumping and field work are typically handled by a septic specialist, and permit rules vary, so ask the Westborough Building Department. If you are unsure of your system, say so on the call.

Digging near a sewer lateral

A repair that needs excavation involves other utilities and local rules. Before anyone digs:

  • Call 811, the Dig Safe line, so buried utilities near the work area are marked.
  • Ask the plumber whether a plumbing permit is needed. Permit rules vary, so ask the Westborough Building Department.
  • Mark sprinkler lines, buried dog fences and anything else you know is underground.
  • Photograph the lawn, driveway and shrubs beforehand so restoration can be compared.
  • Decide where excavated soil can sit for a few days.

Whichever town you live in, the plumbing work follows the Massachusetts Uniform State Plumbing Code, 248 CMR 10.00, and the licensed plumber who does it should be able to explain which parts of the repair fall under it.
